subsection{What the Good--Turing identity predicts}
label{app:gt-identity}
For context $i$, let $p_{iy}$ denote its continuation distribution and
$m_i(F)=sum_y p_{iy}(1-p_{iy})^F$ its expected missing mass after $F$ independent draws. If $K_{i,1}(k)$ is the number of continuation types appearing exactly once in $k$ draws, then
egin{equation}
rac{mathbb E K_{i,1}(F+1)}{F+1}
=sum_y p_{iy}(1-p_{iy})^F=m_i(F).
label{eq:gt-single-context}
end{equation}
This is an expectation identity for a fixed context. In contrast, $K_{i,1}(F)/F$ has expectation $m_i(F-1)$ and equals one at $F=1$ by construction. Neither statement alone identifies the held-out token-weighted missing mass among different contexts observed $F$ times.

paragraph{Heterogeneous contexts and token weighting.}
Assume training context counts $N_i$ are Poisson with means $lambda_i=Tq_i$, continuations are conditionally independent with probabilities $p_{iy}$, and an independent validation token has context distribution $q_i$. Write $pi_F(lambda)=e^{-lambda}lambda^F/F!$. The probability that the continuation of that token is absent from training, conditional on its context having training count $F$, is
egin{equation}
f_star(F)=rac{sum_i q_ipi_F(lambda_i)m_i(F)}{sum_i q_ipi_F(lambda_i)}.
end{equation}
With $n(F)=sum_imathbf1[N_i=F]$ and $N_1(F)=sum_imathbf1[N_i=F]K_{i,1}(F)$, Equation~eqref{eq:gt-single-context} and $(F+1)pi_{F+1}(lambda)=lambdapi_F(lambda)$ give
egin{equation}
egin{split}
mathbb E N_1(F+1)&=Tsum_i q_ipi_F(lambda_i)m_i(F),\
(F+1)mathbb E n(F+1)&=Tsum_i q_ipi_F(lambda_i),\
f_star(F)&=rac{mathbb E N_1(F+1)}{(F+1)mathbb E n(F+1)}.
end{split}
label{eq:gt-poisson}
end{equation}
This is a ratio of expectations under the stated sampling model, not an equality for a realized corpus or the expectation of an empirical ratio. It also need not equal the expected validation fraction conditional on an entire realized training sample. For a bin $B$ of exact counts, a train-only plug-in estimate uses
egin{equation}
widehat f_{mathrm{GT}}(B)=
rac{sum_{Fin B}N_1(F+1)}{sum_{Fin B}(F+1)n(F+1)}.
label{eq:gt-bin}
end{equation}
The weights in this expression are computed from training counts. Weighting exact-layer ratios by held-out token counts instead is a different estimator which uses validation information.

paragraph{Corpus check without calibration.}
Counts use training shard 1 (49,716,936 tokens); the full held-out check uses 49,102,848 prediction positions from shard 2. Context counts follow the recorded raw-concatenation frequency index, whereas validation keys follow the model's 2049-token chunk layout. These conventions include small boundary differences and do not make natural language satisfy the Poisson/i.i.d. assumptions. Figure~ef{fig:b_t_MF} compares Equation~eqref{eq:gt-bin} with direct validation counts, in the 18 native bins from $F=1$ to $1000$. The GT/measured ratios range from 0.870 to 0.885 for bigram and 0.857 to 0.937 for trigram contexts; no validation-fitted scaling factor is applied. At $F=1$ the directly measured fractions are 0.790 and 0.780, respectively, compared with shifted estimates 0.699 and 0.669. The older, unshifted estimator is identically one there.

A within-training-shard split also shows an underestimate: the earlier bigram half/parity-split diagnostic reports ratios 0.861--0.877. That check uses held-out weights within bins, unlike Equation~eqref{eq:gt-bin}. It suggests that train--validation domain shift alone is insufficient to explain the discrepancy. It does not identify within-document repetition as the cause: dependence, nonstationarity, sampling and context heterogeneity have not been separately controlled.

A regularly varying conditional tail can produce slow missing-mass decay: under additional large-$F$, effectively infinite-vocabulary assumptions, $p_{i,(r)}sim A_i r^{-s}$ implies $m_i(F)propto F^{-(1-1/s)}$ citep{gnedin2007occupancy}. This asymptotic statement for one context does not establish a common exponent for a heterogeneous frequency cross-section dominated by small counts. We do not infer the gap exponent from it.

subsection{An idealized crowding-out calculation}
label{app:crowding-derivation}
For a single context with logits $a_v$ and $p_v=softmax(a)_v$, a gradient step directly in logit coordinates on observed target $C$ gives

rac{p_C^{(t)}}{p_u^{(t)}}exp!left[eta(1-p_C^{(t)}+p_u^{(t)})ight].

The exact loss identity is $ell_u-ell_C=log(p_C/p_u)$ for this one distribution. It does not say that the absolute loss of every alternative rises at each step, or that its rise equals the decrease of the target loss; normalization and all other logits also change. Nor is this context-only log-odds difference the bin-level $D_t$ in Equation~eqref{eq:gap-decomp}, which subtracts a separate training average.

For a parameterized network, a small parameter-gradient step instead produces $Delta asimeq-eta JJ^	op(p-e_C)$, with logit Jacobian $J$; an optimizer preconditioner changes this coupling further. Multiple observed targets and shared parameters can therefore alter the direction and depth of individual logit changes. Equations~eqref{eq:av}--eqref{eq:zt} illustrate a sharpening mechanism. They are not a derivation of the measured affine coefficients, context independence, or the complete training dynamics.

subsection{Loss accounting and the role of seen continuations}
label{app:novel-share}
The decomposition in Equation~eqref{eq:gap-decomp} is exact for any bin and any consistently specified scalar training reference. Online and fixed-probe references yield different gaps and different values of $D$ and $S$. A bin's training and validation positions can also contain different contexts and full prefixes; sharing a frequency bin does not match their conditional distributions. Consequently $S$ can be negative, and its magnitude cannot be bounded from an excess-validation-loss decomposition.

paragraph{Scope of the explanation.}
Coverage is a measurable contributor to the frequency dependence, while model- and time-dependent conditional losses set the response. The evidence supports this account on the measured corpus and protocol. It does not prove that coverage is the sole causal mediator, that a shifted GT statistic exactly equals realized novelty, that fitted intercepts measure seen loss, or that a gap exponent is determined by the corpus. Tests across corpora and controlled continuation interventions at fixed count remain useful discriminating experiments.
